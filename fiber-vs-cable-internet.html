Fiber vs. Cable Internet: What Is the Difference?

Editorial guide · Updated October 2026

Fiber and cable are two common types of home broadband, but they work differently and are not available at every address. The right choice depends on which services reach your home, how you use the internet, and the total monthly cost.

How the technologies differ

Fiber internet carries data as light through fiber-optic lines. Fiber plans often offer similar download and upload speeds, which can help with video calls, cloud backups, sending large files, and households with several people online at once.

Cable internet uses cable-TV infrastructure. It can offer fast downloads and is widely available in many areas, but upload speeds may be much lower than download speeds. Actual performance depends on the provider's network and plan.

Speed is only one factor

Do not compare plans using download speed alone. Check upload speed, equipment charges, introductory pricing, the regular price after promotions, data limits if any, installation fees, and whether a contract or early termination fee applies. Ask whether the advertised price requires AutoPay or paperless billing.

Which is better for your household?

  • Frequent video calls or uploading files: fiber may be attractive because many plans provide stronger upload speeds.
  • Streaming and everyday browsing: either technology may be suitable if the plan has enough capacity for the household.
  • Gaming: compare latency and connection consistency as well as speed; a faster advertised download number does not guarantee lower latency.
  • Limited choices: compare the best available cable plan with fiber, fixed wireless, or satellite options offered at your address.

Check availability before deciding

Availability can change from one street or building to the next. Use each provider's official address checker and confirm the exact service type offered at your home. A provider's general coverage map does not guarantee that it can connect a particular address.

Compare the full price

Write down the monthly price during the promotion, the regular price afterward, equipment and installation costs, any required add-ons, and the terms for cancelling. Compare the cost over at least the first 12 months when introductory pricing is involved.
