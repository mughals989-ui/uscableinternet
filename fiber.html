Fiber Internet Providers & Plans

How fiber works, who offers it, what speeds to expect and how to find out if your address can get it.

What is fiber internet?

Fiber internet carries data as pulses of light through strands of glass about as thin as a human hair. Light travels through glass with very little signal loss, so fiber can deliver much higher speeds than copper phone lines or coaxial TV cable, and it keeps those speeds over long distances.

The biggest practical difference you will notice is upload speed. Cable and 5G plans usually upload far slower than they download. Most fiber plans are symmetrical: a 1 Gig plan uploads about as fast as it downloads. That matters for video calls, sending large files, backing up photos to the cloud, streaming to Twitch or YouTube, and working from home.

When people say “fiber,” they usually mean fiber to the home (FTTH), where the glass line runs all the way into your house. Some cable companies advertise a “fiber-powered” network, which usually means fiber runs to your neighborhood and the last stretch to your home is still coaxial cable. That is faster than old networks, but it does not give you true fiber upload speeds.

Pros and cons of fiber internet

Pros

  • Fastest speeds available: plans from 300 Mbps up to multi-gig (2 to 10 Gbps) in some areas.
  • Fast uploads: usually equal to download speed.
  • Low latency: great for online gaming and video calls.
  • Consistent at busy times: less slowdown in the evening than shared cable networks.
  • No data caps with most major fiber providers.

Cons

  • Limited availability: mostly cities and suburbs; many rural homes cannot get it yet.
  • Street-by-street coverage: your neighbor may have fiber while you do not.
  • Installation: usually needs a technician visit, and new lines can take time.
  • Needs power: the fiber terminal and router stop working in a power outage without a battery backup.

How much fiber speed do you need?

Most homes do not need the fastest plan. Pick based on how many people and devices use the internet at the same time:

Plan speedGood for
300 Mbps1 to 3 people: HD and 4K streaming, video calls, browsing, casual gaming
500 Mbps3 to 5 people: several 4K streams at once, working from home, online gaming
1 GigBusy households with many devices, large downloads, smart-home gear, creators uploading video
2 Gig and upPower users, home offices that move huge files, or homes with Wi-Fi 7 equipment that can actually use the extra speed

Wi-Fi is often the real limit. Older routers and phones may not reach multi-gig speeds over Wi-Fi, so before paying for 2 Gig or more, check that your devices and router can use it. For the fastest results, connect gaming consoles and computers with an Ethernet cable.

What to expect when fiber is installed

If your home already has a fiber line from a previous resident, setup can be quick. If not, the provider first runs a line from the street or pole to your house, which may need a separate visit.

On installation day, a technician mounts a small box called an ONT (optical network terminal) inside your home, usually near where the line enters, and connects your Wi-Fi router to it. The visit usually takes a few hours. Ask the technician to place the router somewhere central and open so Wi-Fi reaches the whole house.

Because the ONT and router need power, consider a small battery backup (UPS) if you rely on the internet during power outages.

How to check if fiber is available at your address

Fiber coverage changes from street to street, so a ZIP code alone is not enough.

  1. Use our address checker to see which providers serve your area.
  2. Open each fiber provider's site and enter your full street address to see the exact plans and prices for your home.
  3. Look up your address on the FCC National Broadband Map, which shows the providers and technologies reported for your location.
  4. Read the provider's broadband label before you order. US internet providers must show these labels, which list the monthly price, any promotional price and when it ends, fees, and typical speeds.

Frequently asked questions

What is fiber internet?

Fiber internet sends data as pulses of light through thin glass strands. It delivers very high speeds, low latency and, on most plans, upload speeds as fast as download speeds.

Is fiber internet worth it?

If fiber is available at your address and costs about the same as cable, it is usually the better choice: faster uploads, lower latency, more consistent speeds at busy times and, with most major providers, no data caps.

Is fiber internet available everywhere?

No. Fiber is mostly available in cities and suburbs, and coverage can change from one street to the next. Check your exact street address with each provider or on the FCC National Broadband Map.

Does fiber internet work during a power outage?

Not on its own. The fiber terminal (ONT) and your router need electricity. A battery backup or UPS keeps them running for a while during an outage.

Do fiber plans have data caps?

Most major fiber providers, including AT&T Fiber, Verizon Fios and Google Fiber, do not put data caps on their fiber plans. Always confirm in the provider's broadband label for your address.

How long does fiber installation take?

A typical professional install takes a few hours. If your home has never had fiber, the provider may first need to run a line from the street, which can add days or weeks.
