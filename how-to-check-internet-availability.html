How to Check Internet Availability at Your Address

Editorial guide · Updated October 2026

Internet coverage maps and ZIP-code searches are useful starting points, but they cannot always confirm service at a specific home. Networks may cover only part of a neighborhood, and apartment buildings can have different options from nearby houses.

1. Use your full service address

Enter the street address, apartment or unit number when relevant, city, state, and ZIP code in a provider's official availability checker. If the result is unclear, contact the provider and ask whether the exact address is serviceable.

2. Check more than one technology

Look for fiber, cable, fixed wireless or 5G home internet, and satellite where relevant. Availability and performance differ by location. Do not assume that a provider offering service in your city can connect every home in that city.

3. Verify the exact plan

For each provider that says it serves your address, record the plan name, download and upload speeds, monthly price, promotional period, regular price, equipment charges, installation fees, data policies, and contract terms. Confirm whether taxes and fees are included and whether discounts require AutoPay or paperless billing.

4. Read the offer details

Promotional prices can depend on eligibility, new-customer status, bundles, or payment methods. Review the provider's current broadband label and offer terms where available. Save a copy or screenshot of the offer you intend to order.

5. Compare the total cost

Look beyond the headline monthly price. Add one-time installation and equipment costs, recurring rental charges, and any required services. If the price rises after an introductory period, calculate the total cost over the first year.

6. Confirm installation and cancellation terms

Ask when installation is available, whether a technician visit is required, what equipment is included, and whether the plan has a contract or early termination fee. Check how to return equipment if you later cancel.

What an address checker can and cannot tell you

A comparison website can help you discover providers and navigate to their official sites, but provider databases may change and cannot always guarantee final eligibility. Treat a listing as a starting point and rely on the provider's final address-specific confirmation before placing an order.
