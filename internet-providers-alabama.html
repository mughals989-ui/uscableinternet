Rural Alabama internet

Rural and Black Belt counties often have limited wired service. Check fixed wireless, 5G home, co-ops, then satellite. ADECA coordinates BEAD-funded builds.

Tornadoes and hurricane season

Tornadoes and Gulf storms can take out service. Use a battery backup for the modem and router and a phone hotspot for short outages.

Low-income internet plans in Alabama

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended. Look at Spectrum Internet Assist, AT&T Access and federal Lifeline. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Confirm current rules on the provider’s website.

How to choose a plan in Alabama

1. Check the exact address. City lists are not enough—eligibility is street-level.

2. Match speed to the household. 300–500 Mbps covers most streaming homes; multi-gig fiber helps power users.

3. Prefer fiber when it reaches you. Symmetrical uploads matter for video calls and cloud backups.

4. Read the full price. Compare promo length, equipment fees, taxes and the regular rate after.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet in Alabama?

AT&T Fiber or Google Fiber where built; Spectrum for wide cable; 5G home or Starlink in rural areas. Always check your address.

Is fiber in Birmingham?

In parts of the metro. Confirm with an address check.

What works in rural Alabama?

Fixed wireless, 5G home, regional DSL/fiber and satellite.
