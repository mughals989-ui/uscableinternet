Rural Alaska internet

Outside Anchorage, wired broadband is scarce. Starlink has become the primary high-speed option for many communities. Fixed wireless and 5G home fill some gaps where towers reach.

Winter storms and outages

Alaska winters can disrupt service. Use a battery backup for the modem and router and a phone hotspot for short outages.

Low-income internet plans in Alaska

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended. Look at provider assist plans and federal Lifeline. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Confirm current rules on the provider’s website.

How to choose a plan in Alaska

1. Check the exact address. City lists are not enough—eligibility is street-level.

2. Match speed to the household. 100–300 Mbps covers most streaming homes; higher for power users.

3. Prefer wired or low-latency when available. Satellite latency is higher but workable for most uses.

4. Read the full price. Compare promo length, equipment fees, taxes and the regular rate after.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet in Alaska?

GCI in Anchorage where available; Starlink and 5G home for most of the state. Always check your address.

Is fiber available in Anchorage?

Yes, in parts of the metro via GCI and other builds. Confirm with an address check.

What works in rural Alaska?

Starlink, HughesNet, Viasat and limited fixed wireless or 5G.
