Rural California internet

Mountain, desert and Central Valley towns often have one wired provider or none. Check 5G home internet and fixed wireless first, then satellite. Ask neighbors and your county broadband office about local fiber builds.

Wildfire and power-shutoff season

Planned power shutoffs and wildfires can take down any provider. Put your modem and router on a battery backup and keep a phone hotspot ready for work calls.

Low-income internet plans in California

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at AT&T Access, Xfinity Internet Essentials and Spectrum Internet Assist, plus the federal Lifeline program. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Confirm current rules on the provider's website.

How to choose the right plan in California

Check your exact address. Coverage changes from street to street, even in one city.

Pick speed for your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers or heavy gaming and streaming may need 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when you can get it. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate after.

Frequently asked questions

Is fiber available in Los Angeles and the Bay Area?

In many neighborhoods, yes. AT&T Fiber and Frontier cover parts of Southern California. Fiber is address-specific, so test your exact address.

What is the best internet option in rural California?

Start with 5G home internet if you have a strong signal, then Starlink. Wired options may be limited outside towns.
