Internet Providers in California

California has some of the strongest fiber and cable competition in the country, but it also has remote mountain, desert and farming regions where choices are thin. Providers differ by region: the Bay Area, Los Angeles, San Diego and the Central Valley each have their own mix. This guide covers the main options and how to choose.

Rural California internet

Mountain towns, desert communities and parts of the Central Valley and far north often have fewer wired choices. Start with 5G home internet and fixed wireless providers, then consider satellite if neither works at your address. Local telephone companies and small regional providers sometimes serve areas that national lists miss, so ask neighbors and your county. State and federal broadband funding is expanding service in some regions, but build-out timelines vary.

Wildfires, power shutoffs and staying connected

Wildfires and planned power shutoffs can interrupt internet in California, especially in fire-prone regions. If you work from home or rely on internet for safety, keep a backup such as a 5G home plan or phone hotspot, and use a battery backup for your modem and router. Ask your provider how it handles outages and restoration.

Low-income and discounted internet in California

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at AT&T Access, Spectrum Internet Assist, Xfinity Internet Essentials and Cox Connect2Compete, depending on who serves you. California also runs its California LifeLine program for qualifying households. Eligibility often depends on programs like CalFresh or Medi-Cal. Confirm current rules on each program's site.

How to choose the right plan in California

Check your exact address. Coverage can change from one street to the next, even within the same city.

Match speed to your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers, creators or gamers may want 1 Gig fiber.

Prefer fiber when available. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate afterward.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet provider in California?

It depends on your address. Fiber from AT&T, Frontier or Sonic is best where available. Cable from Spectrum, Xfinity or Cox is the most common alternative.

Which internet providers serve rural California?

Rural areas often use 5G home internet, fixed wireless, local providers or satellite services like Starlink, Hughesnet and Viasat. Check each provider's address tool.

How do wildfires and power shutoffs affect internet?

Planned power shutoffs and fires can cause outages. A 5G backup, a phone hotspot and a battery backup for your router help you stay connected.

Are there discounted internet programs in California?

Yes. Providers offer low-cost plans for qualifying households, and the state runs the California LifeLine program. Eligibility rules apply.
