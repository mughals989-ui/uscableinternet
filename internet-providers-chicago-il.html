Internet Providers in Chicago, IL

Chicago mixes downtown high-rises, brick two-flats and bungalow blocks, and each building type affects which internet service you can get. Cable is available almost everywhere, while fiber and wireless options depend on your street. Here is how the main providers compare across the city.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Plans, prices and availability change often. Confirm with the provider for your address.

Top internet providers in Chicago

Widest availability

Xfinity

The most widely available wired provider in Chicago. Fast cable downloads and many plan tiers, with fiber-based speeds in select areas.

  • Plans from about 150 Mbps up to multi-gig in select areas
  • Frequent new-customer promotions
  • Bundles with mobile and TV are available

Best fiber

AT&T Fiber

Fiber with symmetrical speeds in parts of the city. A strong choice for remote work, video calls and uploading large files.

  • Plans from 300 Mbps up to multi-gig
  • No data caps on fiber plans
  • Coverage varies block by block

Astound Broadband

Formerly RCN, Astound serves parts of Chicago, including many North Side and downtown-area buildings. It can be a good alternative to Xfinity.

  • Cable and fiber plans depending on location
  • Often competes on price in overlap areas
  • Availability is limited to select neighborhoods

Internet by area of Chicago

The Loop and South Loop

High-rise living means your building often decides your options. Xfinity and Astound are common in downtown towers, and many newer buildings include a provider in the rent or HOA. Ask management first.

North Side

Lincoln Park, Lakeview, Uptown and Rogers Park generally have the most competition, with Xfinity, Astound and AT&T Fiber overlapping in many places.

Northwest Side

Logan Square, Irving Park, Portage Park and nearby areas are mostly bungalows and two-flats. Xfinity is widespread and AT&T Fiber is available on many blocks.

West Side

Xfinity is the main wired option in much of the West Side. Check for fiber at your address, and look at discounted plans if your household qualifies.

South Side

Xfinity covers most neighborhoods, with AT&T Fiber reaching some areas. 5G home internet can be a helpful backup where wired choices are limited.

Low-income and student internet in Chicago

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but discounts still exist. Look at Xfinity Internet Essentials and AT&T Access for qualifying households. Chicago families with CPS students should also check Chicago Connected, a city and school program that has provided free internet to eligible households. Program rules and funding change, so confirm current eligibility before you apply.

How to choose the right Chicago plan

Check your exact address. A two-flat on one side of the street can have different options from a courtyard building on the other.

Pick speed for your household. One or two people streaming and browsing do well on 300 to 500 Mbps. Several remote workers, gamers or 4K streams call for 1 Gig or higher.

Ask about winter and outages. A wired connection is usually steadier than wireless. If you rely on internet for work, a cheap 5G plan as backup can be worth it.

Read the price details. Compare the promotional rate, how long it lasts, equipment rental fees and the price after the promo ends.

Renting or in a condo? Ask your landlord or building manager before ordering, since in-unit wiring may need approval.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most available internet provider in Chicago?

Xfinity is the most widely available wired provider across Chicago neighborhoods.

Is fiber internet available in Chicago?

Yes, AT&T Fiber is available in parts of the city, and fiber-based service from other providers reaches select buildings and neighborhoods. Check your exact address.

Can I get free internet for Chicago students?

Chicago Connected is a city and CPS program that has offered free internet to eligible CPS households. Check the program's current status and eligibility.

Is 5G home internet reliable in Chicago?

It works well for many households and makes a good backup, but speeds depend on your block and building, especially in dense high-rise areas.
