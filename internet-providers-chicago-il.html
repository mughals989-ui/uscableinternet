Internet by area of Chicago

The Loop and South Loop

High-rise apartments, condos and converted lofts are common. Your building may already have wiring or an arrangement with a provider. Ask the property manager which services are installed and whether you can choose another provider.

North Side

Lincoln Park, Lakeview, Uptown and Rogers Park include apartments, condos and older residential buildings. Check whether a provider can serve your specific unit and whether the building has installation requirements.

Northwest Side

Logan Square, Irving Park and Portage Park include brick bungalows, two-flats and detached homes. Existing wiring, basement access and the home's connection point can affect installation. Confirm requirements with the provider.

West Side

Housing types and building infrastructure vary across the West Side. Do not assume a service available nearby can also be installed at your home. Compare provider results for your address.

South Side

The South Side includes a wide range of residential buildings and housing arrangements. Check each provider separately, particularly if you are comparing wired service with a 5G home internet option.

Staying connected during Chicago winter weather

Winter storms can bring snow, ice and extreme cold, and severe weather can contribute to power outages. Your modem and router also need electricity to keep your home connected. If you depend on internet for work, school or communication, keep a charged phone or hotspot available and consider a battery backup for networking equipment. Check provider outage information when severe weather affects your area.
