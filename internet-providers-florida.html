Internet Providers in Florida

Florida mixes dense coastal cities, sprawling suburbs, retirement communities and rural interior counties, and the internet options change with each. Condos and HOAs add another twist, and hurricane season makes a reliable backup worth planning for. This guide covers the main providers and how to choose.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Plans, prices and availability change often. Confirm with the provider for your address.

Internet by region of Florida

South Florida

Miami, Fort Lauderdale and Palm Beach have the densest competition, with Xfinity and AT&T Fiber overlapping in many areas. High-rise condos often decide your options by building.

Central Florida

Orlando and surrounding suburbs are mostly Spectrum territory, with AT&T Fiber expanding. Many newer communities have HOA arrangements, so ask before you order.

Tampa Bay

One of the more competitive regions, with Spectrum, AT&T Fiber, Frontier and Fios available in different neighborhoods. Compare offers side by side for your address.

North Florida and the Panhandle

Jacksonville has Xfinity and AT&T Fiber. Smaller towns and the Panhandle may rely more on cable, local providers, 5G home internet and satellite.

Rural interior and the Keys

Fewer wired choices. Start with 5G home internet and fixed wireless, then look at satellite if neither works at your address.

Hurricane season and your internet

Hurricanes and tropical storms can knock out power and networks for days, and no provider is immune. If you work from home or have medical or school needs, prepare a backup before storm season. Good options include a 5G home plan or phone hotspot, a battery backup for your modem and router, and a power bank for your phone. After a storm, report outages to your provider and check its outage map for restoration estimates.

Snowbirds and seasonal residents

If you only live in Florida part of the year, ask your provider whether you can pause service or switch to a lower-cost seasonal option, and read contract terms and early-termination fees before you sign. No-contract 5G home plans are often the easiest to start and stop, and you can usually take the gateway with you.

Condos, HOAs and retirement communities

Many Florida condos, HOAs and retirement communities have bulk agreements with a specific provider, and some include internet in your fees. Ask your association what is already included, which providers are wired into the building, and whether you can add another. Outdoor equipment and installs may need approval from the association.

Low-income internet plans in Florida

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at AT&T Access, Xfinity Internet Essentials and Spectrum Internet Assist, depending on who serves you. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP or Medicaid. Prices and rules change, so confirm on each provider's site.

How to choose the right plan in Florida

Check your exact address. Coverage can differ from one street to the next, and in condos from one building to the next.

Match speed to your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers or heavy streamers may want 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when available. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate afterward.

Plan a backup. A cheap wireless plan can keep you online after a storm.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet provider in Florida?

It depends on your address. Fiber from AT&T, Frontier or Verizon Fios is best where available. Cable from Xfinity or Spectrum is the most common alternative, and 5G home internet or satellite fill in elsewhere.

Do I need a backup for hurricane season?

It is a good idea. Storms can knock out power and networks for days. A 5G home plan or phone hotspot plus a battery backup for your router keeps you more connected.

Can I pause internet service if I only live in Florida part of the year?

Some providers allow vacation holds and others do not. No-contract 5G home plans are often the easiest to start and stop.

Can I choose my internet provider in a Florida condo or HOA?

Not always. Some condos and HOAs have bulk agreements with a specific provider. Ask your association what is included and what you can add.
