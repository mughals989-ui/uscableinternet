Internet Providers in Georgia

Georgia has two very different markets. Metro Atlanta has fiber, cable and wireless competition, while much of rural Georgia depends on local telephone companies, electric cooperatives, wireless or satellite. This guide covers the main providers and how to choose where you live.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Plans, prices and availability change often. Confirm with the provider for your address.

Internet by region of Georgia

Metro Atlanta

The most competitive part of the state. AT&T Fiber, Xfinity and Google Fiber can overlap, so compare them side by side. Apartments and condos in Midtown and Downtown often depend on what the building has wired in.

Atlanta suburbs

Newer neighborhoods in places like Alpharetta, Marietta and Lawrenceville often have both fiber and cable. Some communities have HOA arrangements, so ask before you order.

Savannah and the coast

Cable and fiber are available in the city, with fewer choices in outlying coastal and island areas. A 5G backup is useful during storm season.

Augusta, Macon and Columbus

Mid-sized cities with a mix of cable and fiber. Coverage can change quickly between neighborhoods, so check your exact address.

North Georgia mountains and South Georgia farmland

Fewer wired choices. Look at local telephone companies, electric cooperatives expanding fiber, 5G home internet and satellite.

Rural Georgia internet

Many rural Georgia households have fewer options. Start with 5G home internet and fixed wireless, then look at satellite if neither works at your address. Some rural areas are served by local telephone companies or electric membership cooperatives that have been building fiber, so ask neighbors and your county what exists nearby. State and federal broadband funding is expanding service in some counties, but timelines vary.

Storms and staying connected

Georgia sees severe thunderstorms, tornadoes, tropical storm remnants and occasional ice storms that can cause outages. If you work from home or rely on internet for safety, keep a backup like a 5G home plan or phone hotspot, and use a battery backup for your modem and router. Ask your provider how outages are reported and restored.

Low-income internet plans in Georgia

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at AT&T Access and Xfinity Internet Essentials, depending on who serves you. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP or Medicaid. Prices and rules change, so confirm on each provider's site.

How to choose the right plan in Georgia

Check your exact address. Coverage can change from one street to the next, even inside metro Atlanta.

Match speed to your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers or heavy streamers may want 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when available. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate afterward.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet provider in Georgia?

It depends on your address. Fiber from AT&T or Google Fiber is best where available. Xfinity is the most common cable option in metro Atlanta, and 5G home internet or satellite fill in elsewhere.

Is Google Fiber available in Georgia?

Google Fiber is available in parts of the Atlanta area. Coverage is limited to built-out neighborhoods, so check your exact address.

What internet options exist in rural Georgia?

Rural areas may have local telephone companies, electric cooperatives offering fiber, 5G home internet, fixed wireless or satellite services like Starlink, Hughesnet and Viasat.

Are there low-income internet plans in Georgia?

Yes. AT&T Access and Xfinity Internet Essentials offer discounted plans to qualifying households. Eligibility rules apply.
