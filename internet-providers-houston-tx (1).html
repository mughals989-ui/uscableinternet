Internet Providers in Houston, TX

Houston covers a huge area, from Downtown towers to suburban neighborhoods in Katy, Sugar Land and Spring. The two big wired choices are AT&T Fiber and Xfinity, with 5G home internet filling in the gaps. Here is how they compare and how to pick for your part of town.

Internet by area of Houston

Downtown and Midtown

High-rise apartments and lofts mean building wiring often decides your choice. Ask management which providers are already installed before you order.

Galleria and Uptown

Dense apartments and condos with strong provider competition. Both AT&T Fiber and Xfinity are common, so compare current offers.

The Heights and Inner Loop

Older homes and bungalows can have a mix of fiber and cable. Fiber has reached many Inner Loop streets, so check your exact address.

Katy, Cypress and Sugar Land

Newer suburban neighborhoods often have both fiber and cable. Some communities have builder or HOA agreements, so ask what your community includes.

East End, Pasadena and the North Side

Xfinity is widely available, with AT&T Fiber reaching many areas. Discounted plans for eligible households are worth checking here.

Staying connected during storm season

Houston's hurricane and severe storm season can cause power and network outages, and no provider is immune. If you work from home or depend on internet for medical or school needs, consider a backup like a 5G home plan or a phone hotspot. Keep your router and devices on a surge protector, and ask your provider how outages are reported and restored.

Low-income internet plans in Houston

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still run discounted plans. Look at Xfinity Internet Essentials and AT&T Access for qualifying households. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Prices and rules change, so confirm on the provider's website.

How to choose the right Houston plan

Check your exact address. Coverage changes from one street to the next, and a ZIP code alone is not enough.

Match speed to your household. Streaming and browsing for one or two people works well at 300 to 500 Mbps. Several remote workers, gamers or heavy cloud users should look at 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber for uploads. If you are on video calls all day or send large files, symmetrical speeds make a real difference.

Read the fine print. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate afterward.

Moving into a new build? Ask the builder or HOA about preferred providers and any wiring already in place.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet provider in Houston?

AT&T Fiber is a top choice where it is available thanks to symmetrical speeds. Xfinity is the main cable alternative with wide coverage across the metro area.

Is fiber internet available in Houston?

Yes. AT&T Fiber covers many Houston neighborhoods and suburbs, but availability varies by street. Check your exact address.

What happens to internet during hurricane season?

Storms and power outages can interrupt any provider. A backup option like a 5G home plan or a mobile hotspot can help you stay connected.

Are there low-income internet plans in Houston?

Yes. Xfinity Internet Essentials and AT&T Access offer discounted plans to qualifying households. Eligibility rules apply.
