Internet availability in Houston

The FCC National Broadband Map, data as of June 29, 2026, shows that nearly all residential locations in Houston are covered at 100/20 Mbps or faster, while coverage is lower at 1000/100 Mbps.

These are area-level coverage findings, not confirmation that a particular provider or plan serves an individual home. Availability can vary between nearby addresses and between units in the same building. Check the FCC National Broadband Map for location-level information, then confirm service with the provider.

Internet options by Houston neighborhood

Neighborhood names are a useful starting point, but they cannot confirm which provider serves a particular home. Use the local considerations below as a checklist, then check the full address.

Downtown and Midtown

In high-rise apartments, condos and lofts, the building's existing wiring and provider arrangements can affect which services you can order. Ask the property manager which providers are connected to the building, whether individual units can choose another provider, and whether installation needs building approval.

Galleria and Uptown

For apartments and condominiums around the Galleria and Uptown, check service for the exact building and unit. Ask management about existing wiring, approved installation options and any building-specific provider arrangements before scheduling an installation.

Heights and Inner Loop

Homes in the Heights and across the Inner Loop can have different connection options from one street to the next. When comparing providers, confirm whether fiber or cable reaches the property and whether any installation work is needed at the home.

Katy, Cypress and Sugar Land

For homes in Katy, Cypress and Sugar Land, ask the builder or homeowners association whether the community has a provider agreement, preferred provider, or existing service wiring. Confirm whether residents can choose another provider and check availability at the specific street address.

East End, Pasadena and North Side

Check the exact address with each provider rather than assuming that service in one part of the area extends to another. If a provider cannot serve the property, compare the other wired and wireless options that confirm availability for your location.

Staying connected during Houston storm season

Severe storms can affect electricity, home equipment and communications networks. No internet connection should be assumed to remain available during every storm or power interruption.

  • Ask your provider where to check service alerts and report an outage.
  • Consider a suitable battery backup for your modem and router, while remembering that backup power at home does not prevent an outside network outage.
  • Keep a charged mobile phone and consider a separate mobile-data option for essential communications.
  • Keep networking equipment indoors and follow the equipment manufacturer's safety guidance.
