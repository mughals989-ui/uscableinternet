Compare internet options across Massachusetts

Internet options differ across Greater Boston, Worcester, Springfield, western hill towns and Cape and Islands communities. Compare fiber and cable where available, then consider 5G home internet or fixed wireless if wired plans do not fit your address. In more remote areas, satellite may be another option. Availability varies by street, building and provider network.

Rural Massachusetts internet

Western hill towns and some Cape and Islands communities have limited wired choices. Check local municipal networks, fixed wireless and 5G home, then satellite.

Nor'easters and winter storms

Winter storms and coastal nor'easters can cause outages. A battery backup for the modem and router and a phone hotspot keep you connected during short outages.

Low-income internet plans in Massachusetts

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at Xfinity Internet Essentials, Verizon Fios Forward, Spectrum Internet Assist and the federal Lifeline program. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Confirm current rules on the provider's website.

How to choose the right plan in Massachusetts

Check your exact address. Coverage changes from street to street, even in one city.

Pick speed for your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers or heavy gaming and streaming may need 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when you can get it. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate after.
