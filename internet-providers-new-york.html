Internet options by area in New York

New York City and nearby suburbs: Compare Verizon Fios, Optimum, Spectrum and eligible 5G home internet plans. Apartment and building wiring can affect which providers are available, so check the exact unit address.

Long Island and the Hudson Valley: Availability varies by town and neighborhood. Compare wired service first, then check 5G home internet if you want a wireless alternative.

Buffalo, Rochester, Syracuse and other upstate communities: Spectrum and other wired providers serve many areas, while fiber availability depends on the specific community. Rural and outlying homes may need to compare fixed wireless or satellite.

Use our address checker as a starting point, then verify service directly with the provider. Compare fiber options, 5G home internet and home internet plans before choosing.

Rural New York internet

Upstate and mountain areas have fewer wired choices. Check 5G home and fixed wireless, then satellite.

Winter storms

Ice and snow can interrupt service. A router battery backup and a phone hotspot keep remote work running during short outages.

Low-income internet plans in New York

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at Optimum Advantage Internet, Spectrum Internet Assist, Verizon Fios Forward and the federal Lifeline program. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Confirm current rules on the provider's website.

How to choose the right plan in New York

Check your exact address. Coverage changes from street to street, even in one city.

Pick speed for your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers or heavy gaming and streaming may need 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when you can get it. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate after.
