Internet Providers in North Carolina

North Carolina stretches from the Outer Banks to the Blue Ridge Mountains, and the internet picture changes along the way. Charlotte and the Triangle have strong fiber and cable competition, while mountain valleys and rural counties often rely on local providers, wireless or satellite. Here is how the main options compare.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Plans, prices and availability change often. Confirm with the provider for your address.

Internet by region of North Carolina

Charlotte area

Spectrum and AT&T Fiber overlap across much of the metro, with Google Fiber in select neighborhoods. Compare them for your exact address, especially in newer suburbs.

The Triangle

Raleigh, Durham and Chapel Hill have some of the state's strongest competition, with fiber from AT&T and Google Fiber alongside Spectrum cable.

The Triad

Greensboro, Winston-Salem and High Point are served mainly by Spectrum, with AT&T Fiber in many neighborhoods.

Asheville and the mountains

Terrain makes build-outs harder, so coverage can change a lot between town and valley. Check local providers, 5G home internet and satellite for outlying addresses.

Coast, Outer Banks and Eastern NC

Wilmington and larger coastal towns have cable and some fiber. Smaller communities may rely on local providers, 5G or satellite. Storm resilience matters here.

Mountain and rural internet in North Carolina

Mountain valleys and rural counties have fewer wired choices. Start with 5G home internet and fixed wireless, then consider satellite if neither works at your address. Local telephone companies and electric cooperatives sometimes serve areas national lists miss, so ask neighbors and your county. The state has run broadband grant programs to expand service in underserved areas, but build-out timelines vary by county.

Hurricanes, floods and staying connected

Hurricanes, tropical storms and flooding can damage networks and cut power for days, and the western mountains have seen how severe storm damage can disrupt both wired and wireless service. If you work from home or rely on internet for safety, keep a backup such as a 5G home plan or phone hotspot, use a battery backup for your modem and router, and keep a power bank for your phone. After a storm, report outages and check your provider's outage map.

Low-income internet plans in North Carolina

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at AT&T Access and Spectrum Internet Assist, depending on who serves you. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP or Medicaid. Prices and rules change, so confirm on each provider's site.

How to choose the right plan in North Carolina

Check your exact address. Coverage can differ from one street to the next, even in the same neighborhood.

Match speed to your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers or heavy streamers may want 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when available. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate afterward.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet provider in North Carolina?

It depends on your address. Fiber from AT&T, Google Fiber or Brightspeed is best where available. Spectrum is the most common cable option, and 5G home internet or satellite fill in elsewhere.

What internet options exist in the North Carolina mountains?

Terrain limits wired service in some areas. Options include cable or fiber where built, local providers, 5G home internet, fixed wireless and satellite such as Starlink, Hughesnet and Viasat.

How do hurricanes and floods affect internet in North Carolina?

Storms can damage networks and cut power for days. A 5G backup, a phone hotspot and a battery backup for your router help you stay connected.

Are there low-income internet plans in North Carolina?

Yes. AT&T Access and Spectrum Internet Assist offer discounted plans to qualifying households. Eligibility rules apply.
