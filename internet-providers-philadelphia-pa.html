Internet Providers in Philadelphia, PA

Philadelphia is mostly a two-provider town: Xfinity and Verizon Fios cover most of the city, with 5G home internet as a no-contract alternative. Rowhomes, Center City high-rises and student housing each change what is practical. Here is how the options compare across the city.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Plans, prices and availability change often. Confirm with the provider for your address.

Top internet providers in Philadelphia

Widest availability

Xfinity

Comcast's Xfinity is available across virtually the whole city. Fast cable downloads, many plan tiers and bundles with mobile and TV.

  • Plans from about 150 Mbps up to multi-gig in select areas
  • Frequent new-customer promotions
  • Uploads are slower than fiber on most plans

Internet by area of Philadelphia

Center City and Rittenhouse

High-rises and condos mean your building often decides what is wired in. Ask management whether Xfinity, Fios or both are installed before you order.

South Philadelphia

Dense rowhomes with both Xfinity and Fios available on many blocks. Compare current offers for your exact address, since fiber coverage can vary street by street.

University City and Temple

Student apartments and shared houses dominate. Short leases make no-contract 5G home internet worth a look, and landlords sometimes limit installs.

North and West Philadelphia

Xfinity is widely available, with Fios on many blocks. Discounted plans for eligible households are worth checking here.

Northeast, Roxborough and Manayunk

A mix of single-family homes and rowhouses where both Xfinity and Fios are common. Fiber coverage is strong in many Northeast neighborhoods, so check your address.

Students and short leases

Philadelphia has a large student population, and many students rent shared houses for a year or less. Before you order, ask your landlord whether a provider is already wired in and whether you can install your own equipment. A no-contract 5G home plan avoids early-termination fees and a technician visit, and you can usually take the gateway with you when you move.

Rowhomes and older buildings

Many Philadelphia rowhomes have older wiring. A fiber install may need a technician to run new cable to your unit, and outdoor equipment can need landlord approval. Ask the provider about installation fees and timing, and check whether your building already has a fiber connection you can activate.

Low-income internet plans in Philadelphia

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at Xfinity Internet Essentials and Verizon Fios Forward for qualifying households. Philadelphia has also run local digital equity programs for school families, so ask your school or the city about current options. Prices and rules change, so confirm on each program's site.

How to choose the right Philadelphia plan

Check your exact address. Fios coverage can differ from one block to the next, so a ZIP code is not enough.

Match speed to your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Several remote workers, gamers or 4K streams may want 1 Gig or more.

Prefer fiber if uploads matter. Symmetrical speeds help with video calls and large file transfers.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the price after the promo ends.

Plan for winter and storms. Heavy snow and storm flooding can interrupt service, so a cheap wireless backup is worth considering if you work from home.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main internet providers in Philadelphia?

Xfinity and Verizon Fios are the two main wired providers. T-Mobile Home Internet and Verizon 5G Home are the main wireless options.

Is Verizon Fios available in Philadelphia?

Fios is available across much of the city, but coverage is building by building. Check your exact address with Verizon.

What is the best internet for students in Philadelphia?

Students in apartments often choose between Xfinity, Fios and no-contract 5G home internet. Check lease terms and building wiring first.

Are there low-income internet plans in Philadelphia?

Yes. Xfinity Internet Essentials and Verizon Fios Forward offer discounted plans to qualifying households. Eligibility rules apply.
