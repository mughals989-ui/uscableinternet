Internet Providers in Phoenix, AZ

Phoenix is a fast-growing metro where Cox covers most addresses, fiber is expanding, and 5G home internet is a popular no-contract choice. Heat, monsoon storms and a large seasonal population all shape what works best here. This guide shows the main options and how to choose.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Plans, prices and availability change often. Confirm with the provider for your address.

Top internet providers in Phoenix

Widest availability

Cox

Cox is the main cable provider across the Phoenix area and reaches most neighborhoods. It offers a wide range of speed tiers and a Wi-Fi equipment option.

  • Plans from about 100 Mbps up to multi-gig in select areas
  • Mobile and TV bundles available
  • Uploads are slower than fiber on most plans
Check Cox availability
Best fiber

Quantum Fiber

Fiber service available in parts of the Phoenix metro, with symmetrical speeds that suit remote work and video calls. Brand and ownership changes have been under way, so confirm current plan names.

  • Fiber plans with matching upload and download speeds
  • Coverage varies street by street
  • Check promo length and equipment terms
Check Quantum Fiber availability
No-contract option

T-Mobile Home Internet

Wireless home internet with a plug-in gateway. Popular with renters, short-term residents and anyone who wants a simple backup line.

  • Speeds depend on signal and tower distance
  • Flat monthly pricing
  • Easy to start and stop
Check T-Mobile availability
5G alternative

Verizon 5G Home

Fixed wireless over Verizon's 5G network. A quick self-install that works well in many Phoenix neighborhoods and pairs with a Verizon mobile plan.

  • Self-install in minutes
  • Good for streaming and everyday work
  • Performance varies by location
Check 5G Home availability
Edge-of-town option

Starlink

Satellite internet that helps in outlying desert and foothill areas where wired choices are thin. Equipment needs a clear view of the sky.

  • Useful beyond the wired network
  • Higher equipment and monthly cost than most wired plans
  • Check current availability for your address

Internet by area of Phoenix

Downtown and Midtown

Apartments and condos are common, so your building often decides what is wired in. Ask management which providers are already installed.

Arcadia and Biltmore

Established neighborhoods with a mix of cable and fiber. Compare Cox and fiber offers for your exact address.

Ahwatukee and South Phoenix

Cox is widely available, and fiber has reached parts of the area. Check for discounted plans if your household qualifies.

North Phoenix and Deer Valley

Newer subdivisions often have both cable and fiber. Some communities have HOA arrangements, so ask what yours includes.

West Valley

Growing neighborhoods in areas like Glendale, Peoria and Surprise typically rely on Cox, with fiber and 5G home internet filling in where available.

Seasonal residents and short stays

Many people spend only the winter in Phoenix. If that is you, ask any wired provider whether you can pause service, and read the contract terms before you sign. No-contract 5G home plans are often the easiest to start and stop, and you can usually take the gateway with you.

Heat, monsoon storms and your connection

Keep your router indoors in a cool spot with good airflow, since extreme heat shortens electronics' life. Monsoon storms and dust storms can cause power and network outages, so a backup like a 5G home plan or phone hotspot is worth having if you work from home. A small battery backup helps your modem ride out short outages.

Low-income internet plans in Phoenix

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but some providers still offer discounted plans. Check Cox's Connect2Compete program for eligible households with students, and ask Cox and Quantum Fiber what low-cost plans they currently offer. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP or Medicaid. Prices and rules change, so confirm on the provider's site.

How to choose the right Phoenix plan

Check your exact address. Fiber and cable coverage changes from block to block.

Match speed to your household. 300 to 500 Mbps works for most homes. Several remote workers or heavy streaming may call for 1 Gig.

Choose fiber if uploads matter. Video calls and large uploads benefit from symmetrical speeds.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the price after the promo.

Think about backup. A cheap wireless plan can keep you online when storms hit.
