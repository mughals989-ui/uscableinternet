Internet Providers in Texas

Texas is huge, and your internet options depend on where you live. Big metros like Houston, Dallas, Austin and San Antonio have fiber and cable competition, while rural counties often rely on wireless or satellite. This guide covers the main provider types and how to choose in your part of the state.

Compare internet options across Texas

When comparing internet service in Texas, start with the connection types available at your exact address. Fiber is worth considering for fast uploads and video calls; cable can offer high download speeds in many metro areas; 5G home internet may be convenient where the signal and plan eligibility are good; and satellite can help in remote communities with limited wired coverage.

Before choosing a plan, compare the regular monthly price after promotions, equipment charges, upload speeds, data policies and any contract or cancellation terms. Provider coverage varies by neighborhood, so a provider's presence in a city does not guarantee service at every address.

Rural Texas internet

Many rural Texas households have fewer wired choices. Start by checking 5G home internet and fixed wireless providers, then look at satellite if neither works at your address. Some communities are served by local telephone companies or electric co-ops that offer fiber, so ask your neighbors and your county's broadband office what exists nearby. Public broadband funding is expanding service in some counties, but timelines vary.

Staying connected in storm and heat season

Texas hurricanes, winter storms and summer heat can cause outages that affect any provider. If you work from home, consider a 5G home plan or mobile hotspot as a backup, and put your modem and router on a battery backup so they stay on during short power losses.

Low-income internet plans in Texas

The federal Affordable Connectivity Program has ended, but providers still offer discounted plans. Look at AT&T Access, Xfinity Internet Essentials and Spectrum Internet Assist depending on your area. Eligibility often depends on programs like SNAP, Medicaid or SSI. Confirm current rules on the provider's website.

How to choose the right plan in Texas

Check your exact address. Coverage can change from street to street, even within the same city.

Choose speed for your household. 300 to 500 Mbps suits most homes. Multiple remote workers or heavy gaming and streaming may need 1 Gig.

Prefer fiber when you can get it. It is the most consistent option for uploads and video calls.

Read the price details. Compare the promo price, how long it lasts, equipment fees and the regular rate after.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best internet provider in Texas?

It depends on your city and street. Fiber from AT&T, Google Fiber or Frontier is best where available. Cable from Xfinity or Spectrum is the most common alternative.

What internet options exist in rural Texas?

Rural areas often rely on 5G home internet, fixed wireless, local fiber co-ops or satellite services like Starlink, Hughesnet and Viasat. Check each provider's address tool.

Is fiber available in Texas?

Yes, in many Texas metro areas and suburbs. Fiber coverage varies by neighborhood, so check your exact address.

How can I keep internet during Texas storms?

No provider is immune to outages. A 5G home plan, a mobile hotspot and a battery backup for your router are practical ways to stay connected.
