Which internet connection fits you?

Start with what matters most in your home.

  • Fiber for the fastest speeds and equal upload speeds.
  • Cable for wide availability and low starting prices.
  • 5G home for no-wire setup and a trial period.
  • Satellite when no wired service reaches your address.

Compare more than the advertised monthly price

The lowest starting price is not always the lowest total cost. Before signing up, compare the regular price after promotions, equipment charges, installation fees, contract terms and any price guarantee. Check the provider's broadband label for the exact address because offers and typical speeds can vary by location.

For working from home

Look for reliable upload speeds and low latency for video calls, VPNs and sending large files. Fiber is often the strongest fit where available; cable can also work well.

For streaming and families

Estimate how many people will stream, game and join video calls at the same time. Compare the plan's typical speeds and check whether the gateway or router is included.

For rural addresses

Check fixed wireless first if local signal is strong. If wired and cellular options are unavailable, compare satellite equipment costs, latency, data policies and installation requirements.

Provider coverage differs by city, neighborhood and even street. Browse internet providers by state or use the address checker before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Which internet type is fastest?

Fiber offers the fastest speeds in this comparison, up to 1,000 Mbps on AT&T and 940 Mbps on Verizon Fios, with equal upload speeds.

Is cable or fiber better for home internet?

Fiber has faster uploads and lower latency. Cable is widely available and often cheaper to start. Check both at your address.

Is 5G home internet a good option?

T-Mobile 5G Home starts at $35 per month with a 15-day test drive and self-install. Speeds are 72-245 Mbps and depend on your signal.

What if I live in a rural area?

Satellite internet from HughesNet and Starlink is available coast to coast. Starlink lists 50-220 Mbps.
