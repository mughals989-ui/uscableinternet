Satellite Internet: Plans, Speeds and Costs

Editorial guide · Updated November 18, 2026

Satellite internet can serve rural homes where fiber, cable and fixed wireless are unavailable. Compare hardware cost, monthly price, latency and data policies before you order.

What to compare

  • Equipment and installation fees
  • Typical download and upload speeds
  • Latency for video calls and gaming
  • Data thresholds or deprioritization rules

Starlink Residential plan options · checked November 18, 2026

Starlink currently lists three U.S. Residential options: Residential 100 Mbps, Residential 200 Mbps and Residential Max. Its official site asks customers to enter their address to see residential plan pricing and the hardware cost, so there is no single nationwide monthly quote shown here.

Residential 100 Mbps and 200 Mbps are limited to their named download speeds and are available only in select areas. Residential Max offers the maximum available speeds for the location. Starlink says speeds are not guaranteed and may be slower during congestion. Residential plans include unlimited data. Confirm the monthly price, kit cost, installation and any local offer in the official address-specific checkout.

Frequently asked questions

Is satellite internet good for rural homes?

It can be a practical option where fiber, cable, and fixed wireless are unavailable. Compare available speeds, equipment costs, latency, and service terms at your exact address.

Does weather affect satellite internet?

Heavy rain, snow, and severe weather can affect satellite signals or equipment performance. The impact depends on the service, dish installation, and local conditions.

Is satellite internet suitable for gaming and video calls?

Modern low-Earth-orbit services can support many calls and online games, but latency and performance may vary. Traditional geostationary satellite services typically have much higher latency.

Does unlimited satellite internet mean no limits?

Not always. Review network-management, priority-data, and acceptable-use policies because speeds may change during congestion even when a plan advertises unlimited data.
