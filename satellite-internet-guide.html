Satellite Internet: What to Know Before Ordering

Editorial guide · Updated October 2026

Satellite internet can connect homes where cable and fiber networks are unavailable, including some rural and remote locations. Different satellite services have different equipment, performance, pricing, and coverage, so compare the actual offer for your address.

Understand the technology

Satellite systems communicate with equipment at your home and satellites in orbit. Newer low-Earth-orbit systems can have lower latency than traditional geostationary systems, but performance still depends on the service, location, network demand, and view of the sky.

Check installation requirements

Ask where the dish or antenna must be placed, whether trees or buildings can block its view, whether professional installation is required, and whether you need to purchase or rent equipment. Check any mounting, power, or obstruction requirements before ordering.

Read usage and pricing terms

Compare equipment costs, installation, monthly price, taxes and fees, data policies, congestion-related restrictions, and cancellation terms. Advertised speeds are not guaranteed at every moment, so review the provider's stated performance expectations and support options.

Is satellite the best choice?

It may be a useful option when terrestrial broadband is not available. If fiber, cable, or fixed wireless is available at a similar cost, compare latency, upload speeds, data policies, and total ownership cost before choosing.

Start by checking local internet options and confirming service on the provider's official site.

Provider prices, terms, speeds, and availability can change. Confirm current details directly with the provider before ordering.
