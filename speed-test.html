What your speed test measures

  • Download speed: How quickly your connection receives data. It affects activities such as streaming, browsing and downloading files.
  • Upload speed: How quickly your connection sends data. It matters for video calls, sending large files, cloud backups and live streaming.
  • Ping or latency: The time it takes data to travel to a test server and back. Lower latency generally feels more responsive, especially in online games and live calls.
  • Jitter: How much latency changes over time. Large fluctuations can make real-time calls and games less consistent.

How to get a more reliable result

  1. Pause downloads, cloud backups, streaming and other heavy network activity on your devices.
  2. If possible, connect your computer to the router with an Ethernet cable. Wi-Fi results can vary with distance, walls, interference and the device you use.
  3. If you need to test over Wi-Fi, move close to the router and use the same device and location for repeat tests.
  4. Run the test more than once, at different times of day. Internet traffic and network congestion can affect results.
  5. Temporarily disconnect a VPN if you want to measure the connection without its additional routing. Only do this if it is safe and appropriate for your network.

How to understand your results

Compare your download and upload results with the speeds advertised for your current plan, but do not expect every test to match the plan's maximum advertised rate. Actual performance can vary by connection type, network conditions, Wi-Fi equipment, device capability and the test server.

If results are consistently lower than expected, repeat the test with a wired connection, restart your modem and router, and check whether the issue affects more than one device. If the problem continues, contact your provider and share the test time, results and whether you used Wi-Fi or Ethernet.

Is your internet plan fast enough?

Your ideal speed depends on how many people and devices use the connection at the same time, and what they do online. Video streaming, video meetings, gaming, large downloads and smart-home devices all compete for available bandwidth. A household with several simultaneous users may need more capacity than someone who mainly browses and checks email.

Use our guide to how much internet speed you need to think through your household's usage. Then compare home internet plans or check providers at your address. Provider availability, pricing and actual speeds vary by location; confirm the details directly with the provider before ordering.
