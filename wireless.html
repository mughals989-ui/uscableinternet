What is 5G home internet?

5G home internet, also called fixed wireless, gets your home online through a cell phone network instead of a cable or fiber line. The provider sends you a small gateway (a modem and Wi-Fi router in one). You plug it in near a window, it picks up the 5G signal from a nearby tower, and it creates a Wi-Fi network for your phones, TVs, laptops and game consoles.

There is no technician visit and no digging. Most people are online within an hour of opening the box. Because it uses the same towers as cell phones, 5G home internet is often available in places that cable and fiber do not reach, including many small towns and suburbs.

It is different from using your phone as a hotspot. Home internet plans come with a dedicated gateway with stronger antennas, unlimited home data and pricing built for a whole household.

Pros and cons of 5G home internet

Pros

  • Easy self-setup: plug in the gateway, no installer needed.
  • Simple pricing: flat monthly price, equipment usually included.
  • No annual contract on the main plans, and some offer a trial period.
  • Unlimited data with T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T home plans.
  • Bundle discounts if you already have a phone plan with the same carrier.
  • Easy to move: update your address and take the gateway with you (where the new address is eligible).

Cons

  • Speeds vary: depends on signal strength, walls, weather and tower traffic.
  • Slower uploads than fiber.
  • Can slow at busy times: carriers may prioritize phone traffic over home internet when a tower is crowded.
  • Higher lag than fiber or cable, which matters for competitive gaming.
  • Limited sign-ups: carriers cap customers per tower, so your address may be waitlisted.

Who should choose 5G home internet?

A good fit if you: mostly stream, browse and make video calls; want a lower bill than cable; rent or move often; already have a phone plan with T-Mobile, Verizon or AT&T; or live where cable and fiber are slow, expensive or unavailable.

Think twice if you: play competitive online games, upload large files every day, run a busy home office, or have a weak cell signal at home. In those cases fiber or cable will usually be more reliable. If no wired service or 5G reaches you, look at satellite options such as Starlink.

How to get the best speed from 5G home internet

  1. Place the gateway near a window, ideally on an upper floor and on the side of your home facing the nearest tower.
  2. Use the carrier's app. T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T apps show signal strength and help you find the best spot.
  3. Keep it clear of metal, thick walls, mirrors and other electronics.
  4. Add a mesh Wi-Fi system if the best signal spot is far from where you use the internet most.
  5. Test at different times of day with our speed test, especially in the evening, before you cancel your old service.

How to check if 5G home internet is available at your address

Availability depends on your exact address, the signal at your building and how many customers the local tower already serves.

  1. Use our address checker to see which providers serve your area.
  2. Enter your full street address on the T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T Internet Air sites. If your address is not eligible yet, join the waitlist; space can open up as networks expand.
  3. Compare the provider's broadband label for your address. Every US internet provider must show one, listing the monthly price, promotional terms, fees and typical speeds.

Frequently asked questions

What is 5G home internet?

5G home internet uses a cell phone network instead of cables. A small gateway in your home picks up the 5G signal and turns it into Wi-Fi for all your devices.

Is 5G home internet as good as cable?

With a strong signal, 5G home internet is fast enough for streaming, video calls and working from home, and it is often cheaper. Cable and fiber are usually more consistent, with faster uploads and lower lag for gaming.

Does 5G home internet have data caps?

T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T Internet Air home plans include unlimited data. During busy times, home internet traffic may be slowed before phone traffic on the same network.

Can I try 5G home internet before committing?

Yes. These plans are month to month with no annual contract, and T-Mobile offers a 15-day trial. Test it in your home before cancelling your current service.

Is 5G home internet good for gaming?

It works for casual gaming, but lag can rise when the network is busy. Competitive gamers usually do better with fiber or cable.

Why can my neighbor get 5G home internet but I cannot?

Carriers limit how many home internet customers each cell tower can serve, and signal strength varies by building. Availability is checked address by address and can open up later.
